Three ways to put both cameras on the Lite 6

Printable brackets for the Arducam B0495 and the RealSense D405 on the Lite 6 tool flange, gripper off. All three share one flange adapter; only the camera head differs. Below is what each head does to the arm on the weld-inspection passes, and how to print and fit it.

Print Twin first. It is the simplest and lightest, holds the cameras closest to the flange, and keeps J5 at 47–91° with the T-joint placed web-toward-the-robot (the layout the setup figure settled on).
Switch to Angled 45° if the weld has to face away from the robot. It is the only head that stays clear of the J5 singularity both ways.
Inline puts the TCP on the J6 axis, so roll is free, at the cost of 44 mm more reach and J5 within 9° of its limit.

The designs

Every head bolts to the same adapter with 4 × M3 into heat-set inserts, so you can print more than one and swap. Numbers come from the FreeCAD models and the Lite 6 kinematic model used for the setup figure.

J5 along the weld

The wrist angle J5 at every point along the seam (horizontal axis, mm from its middle) on each of the three passes. Near 0° the wrist is singular: J4 and J6 swing wildly and the arm loses accuracy. The project keeps |J5| ≥ 10°; the limit is 124°.

Print and fit

Printing

  • Material: PETG, or ASA if the cell gets warm. PLA creeps under the screw clamp.
  • Settings: 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 5 walls, 5 top and 5 bottom layers, 40% gyroid.
  • Orientation: the STLs come oriented as they should print, back face on the bed. No part needs supports: nothing overhangs past 45°.
  • Two spots to watch: Inline's deck is a 39 mm bridge between its walls, and Angled's deck underside is exactly 45°. Both print clean in PETG with bridging on. If your slicer adds supports inside them, block those.
  • Per bracket: one adapter, one centring ring, one head. The adapter and ring serve every head.
  • Check the fit first: print the ring alone and try it in the flange's Ø31.5 recess. If it's tight, scale it 99.5% in XY.

Hardware, per bracket

  • 4 × M6 × 12 socket-head screws, adapter to flange, on the 50 mm bolt circle. The Lite 6 manual's End Flange section gives 6 × M6 plus the Ø6 locating hole, and the gripper fits with M6 bolts; ignore the "(M5×6)" in its spec summary table.
  • 1 × Ø6 × 16 steel dowel pin, pressed into the adapter; 6 mm enters the flange's locating hole. Printed holes come out a little small, so open it with a 6 mm drill by hand if the pin won't go.
  • 4 × M3 heat-set inserts (M3 × 5.7) and 4 × M3 × 8 socket-head screws, head to adapter.
  • 4 × M2 × 6 thread-forming screws, Arducam to its posts (Ø1.75 pilot holes).
  • 1 × 1/4"-20 × 3/8" screw for the D405's tripod hole, 8.35 mm from its back face (Intel D400 datasheet, Figure 10-13). The camera allows 5 mm of thread; the ledge is 5 mm thick.
  • A straight USB-C cable for the Arducam and a Micro-B cable for the D405, tied down the arm's far side.

Fitting it, in this order

  1. Press the dowel into the adapter from the back and push the centring ring into its pocket. Melt the four M3 inserts into the front.
  2. Take the gripper off. Seat the adapter, ring in the flange recess and dowel in the Ø6 hole, and bolt it down with the four M6 screws.
  3. Bolt the head on with the four M3 screws. The hex key reaches each one through its access hole; do this before the cameras, which cover some holes.
  4. Fit the D405 on its ledge with the 1/4"-20 screw from underneath, its back flat against the head.
  5. Screw the Arducam to its four posts with its USB-C edge, the bottom of the board, over the slot: outboard on Twin, toward the tower's open side on Inline, up the slope on Angled. The plug goes straight in through the slot.
  6. Refocus the Arducam lens for the working distance; it ships focused at 2 m to infinity.
  7. Set the payload in the controller, then run calibration.capture and calibration.solve for the new hand-eye transform.

Questions to answer before printing

  1. What screw fits the Arducam's four mounting holes: M2, M2.5 or M3? Measure one hole: about 2.2 mm means M2, 2.7 mm M2.5, 3.2 mm M3. The posts' pilot holes are sized for M2.
  2. What does the Arducam weigh with its lens? The payload figures assume 30 g.
  3. What working distance will you use? The 120 mm standoff is still an open question. The TCP moves with it, and the hand-eye calibration measures the real one anyway.

Reach numbers come from an ideal model of the Lite 6. Check the passes with tools/plan_check.py on the controller's own IK before running them.
